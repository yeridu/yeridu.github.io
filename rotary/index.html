What we are trying to change

The risks arrive together. The services do not.

In Toledo
  • Toledo has the highest level of multidimensional poverty of any district in Belize
  • Nationally, 35.7% of people lived in multidimensionally poor households in September 2021
  • Poverty in rural communities runs at double the urban rate

Statistical Institute of Belize, Multidimensional Poverty Index

What the research finds
  • A 2024 qualitative study of gender-based violence in Toledo, drawing on clinic staff, describes control, power and patriarchy as running themes
  • Suicide attempts and suicide mortality in Belize have been tracked nationally across 2019 to 2023

Kirsch et al., J Fam Violence, 2024 · Oladeji et al., IJCMPH, 2025

What TCC students told us
  • An earlier student survey at TCC found elevated emotional distress, limited reproductive health knowledge, substance use and exposure to violence
  • No standardised curriculum currently addresses these areas at the school

Before we build anything

How to tell a serious prevention programme from a poster campaign

4 Design
  • Start from local realities, not a generic template
  • Address harmful gender norms directly
  • Build skills step by step, not all at once
  • Address multiple causes of violence together
2 Delivery
  • Train and support strong facilitators
  • Follow the programme, but adapt it to the culture
4 Infrastructure
  • Session plans with clear goals
  • Safe-space rules and trauma awareness
  • Tools to track who attends and what changes
  • A referral plan for when someone needs help

A one-off assembly changes nothing. Norm change needs repetition, practice and reinforcement over time.

Plain-language summary drawing on Jewkes et al. 2021, "Elements of the design and implementation of interventions to prevent violence against women and girls associated with success" (Int J Environ Res Public Health 18:12129).

TIME 6:00-8:00. This is the most directly useful slide for this audience and you should say so: these ten points are a screening tool. Any club that reviews grant requests can use them to ask harder questions of anyone -- including me -- who comes asking for money to run a programme for young people. Invite them to hold THRIVE to it. That earns more trust than any claim you can make about your own work.
The curriculum

Seven modules, three clusters, one system

3 Self
  • Emotions and communication
  • Mental and physical health
  • Substance use and refusal skills
2 Relationships
  • Healthy relationships and assertiveness
  • Sexual and reproductive health
2 Community
  • Masculinities and boys' health
  • Environmental health
30 classroom sessions plus 1 booster 40 minutes each Delivered by trained teachers Plus whole-school components: school mapping, an anonymous speak-out box, a School Health Promotion Committee, and school health policies
TIME 8:00-10:00. Do not read all seven modules aloud -- let the slide do it. Make two points instead. First, the three clusters move outward: self, then relationships, then community. Second, it is not only a classroom curriculum; the whole-school pieces are what make it stick. If someone asks why a health curriculum includes environmental health, the answer is that in Toledo, flooding, water and land pressure shape what families can do -- and the environment shapes the norms young people grow up inside.

The part most programmes leave out

We wrote down, in advance, what would make us stop.

Green — proceed

Acceptability, appropriateness and feasibility all average 3.5 or above out of 5; at least 60% of teachers willing to deliver it; no fundamental safeguarding barrier.

Amber — proceed only with changes

Scores between 3.0 and 3.5, or concerns that can genuinely be fixed by adapting the curriculum or strengthening training.

Red — do not proceed

Scores below 3.0, teacher willingness below 40%, substantial community opposition, or safeguarding capacity that is not adequate.

These thresholds were set before we collected a single response, and they are published. If Toledo says no, the answer is no.

TIME 13:30-15:00. This is your credibility slide. Say it in plain terms: we published the conditions under which we would abandon our own project, before we had any data, so that we could not move the goalposts afterwards. "Substantial community opposition" is a stopping condition in writing. Then make the direct offer: this club is part of the community whose opposition would count. If you think this is wrong for Toledo, I would rather hear it from you at this table than find out after we have spent two years on it.
In your language

The Four-Way Test, applied to this study

Is it the TRUTH?

The study is registered publicly and the analysis plan is fixed in advance. We report what we find, including a result that says this curriculum is not wanted here.

Is it FAIR to all concerned?

Active written parental consent, then written youth assent. Anyone may skip a question or withdraw. Materials in English, Spanish and Q'eqchi'/Mopan Maya.

Will it build GOODWILL and BETTER FRIENDSHIPS?

The curriculum was shaped by TCC leadership, teachers and this community. Every participant receives a plain-language summary of the findings, and the ministries receive full reports.

Will it be BENEFICIAL to all concerned?

That is precisely the question Phase 1 exists to answer — and the progression criteria are what stop us assuming the answer is yes.

TIME 15:00-16:30. Do not perform this or turn it into flattery -- Rotarians recite the Four-Way Test at meetings and will notice if you are using it decoratively. Use it as a genuine structure: research ethics and the Four-Way Test are asking the same four questions in different vocabularies. The strongest line is under TRUTH: we will publish a negative result. Most people who present to service clubs never mention the possibility that their idea does not work.
The question you should ask me

"What happens when a child tells you something serious?"

The 4Rs — the NSPCC safeguarding approach we follow
  • Recognise — know what a disclosure or an indicator looks like
  • Respond — respond safely in the moment, without promising secrecy we cannot keep
  • Report — escalate to the Designated Safeguarding Lead and, where required, to the authorities
  • Record — write it down accurately, at the time
What that means in practice here
  • Every disclosure triaged as immediate, urgent or routine
  • Expressed suicidal thinking triggers the Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale and a defined response pathway
  • Staff trained in safeguarding, C-SSRS, cultural sensitivity and human-subjects research before any contact
  • Limits of confidentiality stated up front, including mandatory reporting under the Belize Families and Children Act
  • Referral pathways agreed with local services in advance
TIME 16:30-18:00. Answer this before anyone has to ask it -- that is the whole point of putting it on a slide. Be concrete and unemotional. Two things this room will care about most: first, we never promise a young person secrecy we are not allowed to keep, and we tell them that before they speak; second, mandatory reporting under the Belize Families and Children Act applies to us like anyone else. If a Rotarian asks whether referral capacity in Toledo is actually adequate to absorb what we might surface, do not defend -- that is a real limitation, it is one of the things Phase 1 is assessing, and saying so is the honest answer.

What I am actually asking for

Four things, and none of them is money today.

  • 01 Sit with us as community stakeholders. The protocol includes twelve community stakeholders with recognised adolescent-health expertise living in Toledo District. Members of this club qualify. Participation is voluntary, with informed consent, under the approved protocol.
  • 02 Consider chartering an Interact club at TCC. Interact is Rotary's own programme for young people aged 12 to 18 — exactly the ages in Forms 1 to 4 at TCC. It is sponsored by a Rotary club, self-governing, and runs two service projects a year. It is entirely independent of this study, and it would outlast it.
  • 03 Tell us what we are getting wrong. Scheduling around the school year and harvest. Language, across English, Spanish, Q'eqchi' and Mopan. How to reach parents who will not come to a meeting at the school. And what has already been tried in Toledo and failed, so we do not repeat it.
  • 04 Later, and only if Phase 1 says proceed. If the findings support going forward, we would welcome a conversation about whether a Rotary district or global grant is a fit, given how the modules line up with the areas of focus. Not today.
TIME 18:00-19:30. Lead with the sentence on the heading: none of these is money today. Then slow down on number two. Interact is Rotary's programme, not yours -- you are not asking them to adopt your idea, you are pointing out that a thing they already know how to do fits a school you already fund scholarships into, and it survives whatever happens to this study. Number three is not a courtesy; write down what they say. On number four, be disciplined: name it, say "not today," and move on. If you push for funding at a first visit you will lose the other three.
