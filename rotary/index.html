What we are trying to change

The risks arrive together. The services do not.

1The conditions in Toledo
  • Toledo has the highest level of multidimensional poverty of any district in Belize
  • Nationally, 35.7% of people lived in multidimensionally poor households in September 2021
  • In rural communities the rate runs at about double the urban rate

Statistical Institute of Belize, Multidimensional Poverty Index

→so researchers asked what this does to families here
2What the research finds
  • A 2024 study of gender-based violence in Toledo District reported three themes: what drives the violence; control, power and patriarchy; and how women respond to it
  • Belize's suicide rate rose from 6.99 to 11.38 per 100,000 people between 2019 and 2023, and suicide attempts are most common among adolescents aged 15 to 19 — the ages in Forms 1 to 4 at TCC

Kirsch et al., J Fam Violence, 2024 (interviews with health workers in Toledo District) · Oladeji et al., IJCMPH, 2025

→so we asked TCC's own students, and counted the answers
3What TCC students told us
  • Emotional distress. 32.7% had harmed themselves and 26.6% had attempted suicide. 45.1% said they rarely or never got the help they needed.
  • Reproductive health. 63.2% could not say where to get contraception, rising to 75.5% among girls. 20.8% had never heard of HIV.
  • Substance use. 33.8% had vaped. 25.1% drank alcohol in the past 30 days.
  • Violence. 55.0% had seen violence in their neighbourhood. 43.6% had been bullied at school.
  • In May 2025 no standardised curriculum at the school addressed any of these areas. That is still true today.

THRIVE-Belize needs assessment, Toledo Community College, May 2025. Forms 1-3, 86.5% of the school took part, 497 students analysed.

Poverty and distance from services set the conditions. Published research describes what happens to families under those conditions. In May 2025 we measured it at this school, student by student.

Before we design anything

Ten elements separate prevention that works from prevention that does not.

Video unavailable offline. The ten elements are listed beside it — nothing is lost.

Five minutes on what the evidence actually says. The list beside it is the same list, so the room can follow either way.

One assembly changes nothing. Changing a norm takes repetition, practice and reinforcement over time.

The ten elements and their three groupings are taken from Jewkes R, Willan S, Heise L, et al. Elements of the design and implementation of interventions to prevent violence against women and girls associated with success. Int J Environ Res Public Health 2021;18:12129. doi:10.3390/ijerph182212129. The wording is simplified for a general audience; the line beside each element is ours, not the paper's.

4 Design — essential
  1. A plan built on a clear theory of change, fitted to the local contextTHRIVE: designed from a survey of TCC's own students, with school climate as the mechanism
  2. Address several drivers of violence togetherTHRIVE: seven modules working as one system, not a single topic
  3. Support for survivorsTHRIVE: the NSPCC 4Rs, a suicide-risk response pathway, referrals agreed with local services in advance
  4. Work with men and women, boys and girlsTHRIVE: whole-class delivery, plus a module on masculinities and boys' health
2 Implementation — essential
  1. Enough intensity for people to reflect and practiseTHRIVE: 30 sessions of 40 minutes plus a booster, across the school year
  2. Enough well-selected, trained and supported staffTHRIVE: structured teacher training before any delivery. Phase 1 tests whether TCC can staff it
4 Where relevant to the approach
  1. Group activities that build empowerment and good relationshipsTHRIVE: every session is activity-based, not a lecture
  2. Participatory learning: critical reflection and communication skillsTHRIVE: debates, role-play and storytelling in each module
  3. A clear, user-friendly manual, followed systematicallyTHRIVE: a written facilitator manual covering all 30 sessions
  4. Age-appropriate design with an engaging pedagogy, for childrenTHRIVE: written for Forms 1 to 4, ages 12 to 17

Why these areas, in Rotary's own words

Peacebuilding and conflict prevention covers "education for youth on constructive ways to prevent, manage, and transform conflict" and "facilitated dialogue, communication". Disease prevention and treatment covers "prevention and treatment services for physical and mental illnesses". Maternal and child health covers "education about and enhanced access to family planning and other sexual and reproductive health interventions" and "interventions to reduce the transmission and impact of sexually transmitted disease in adolescents".

Two limits we will not talk around

The same policy excludes "general health education" from disease prevention, and it funds adolescent sexual and reproductive health work only when it is carried out inside the health care system under licensed medical supervision. That is why Hillside Health Care is a partner in this study rather than an afterthought. And basic education and literacy is not on this list: it covers literacy, numeracy and teacher development, so the honest fit there is our teacher training, not the content of any module.

Areas of focus and the quoted scope are from The Rotary Foundation, Areas of Focus Policy Statements (October 2020). Prevalences are from the THRIVE-Belize needs assessment at TCC, May 2025. The mapping between the two is ours, not Rotary's, and we are happy to be argued with on any row.

The part most programmes leave out

We wrote down, in advance, what would make us stop.

How one score is made
1
Everyone answers on the same 5-point scale.
1 Strongly disagree2 Disagree3 Not sure4 Agree5 Strongly agree
2
We average one person's answers within one section.
A real example — the four acceptability statements on the student survey
"I would be interested in lessons that help students handle stress and strong feelings."Agree · 4
"I would like lessons about healthy friendships and dating relationships."Strongly agree · 5
"I would like lessons that teach how to make safer choices about alcohol and drugs."Agree · 4
"I would like lessons about how to take care of our sexual and reproductive health."Not sure · 3
(4 + 5 + 4 + 3) ÷ 4 = 4.0 — this student's acceptability score
3
We average those person scores across the whole group.

That group average is the number the traffic light reads. The same three sections are scored the same way for students, parents, teachers, the principal, school staff and local informants: acceptability (do you want it?), appropriateness (does it fit here?) and feasibility (can this school actually run it?).

Green — proceed

3.5 or above on all three sections — the average answer sits between "Agree" and "Strongly agree". Plus at least 60% of teachers willing, and no fundamental safeguarding barrier.

Amber — proceed only with changes

3.0 to 3.5 — the average sits between "Not sure" and "Agree". We may proceed only if the concerns can genuinely be fixed by adapting the curriculum or strengthening the training.

Red — do not proceed

Below 3.0 — the average is at "Not sure" or worse. Also red: teacher willingness below 40%, substantial community opposition, or safeguarding capacity that is not adequate.

These thresholds were set before we collected a single response, and they are published. If Toledo says no, the answer is no.

TIME 14:00-16:30. This is the credibility slide, so do not rush the arithmetic. Walk the left column in order: one scale, one average per person, one average per group. Read the four student statements out loud -- they are the actual printed items, and hearing them tells the room exactly what we are asking their children. Then move right and translate each threshold back into words: green means the average person agreed; amber means the average person was somewhere between not sure and agreeing; red means the average person was not sure or worse. Then say the point of the whole slide: we published the conditions under which we would abandon our own project, before we had any data, so that we could not move the goalposts afterwards. "Substantial community opposition" is a stopping condition in writing, and this club is part of the community whose opposition would count. If you think this is wrong for Toledo, we would rather hear it at this table than find out after two years.

One module, from the inside

Masculinities and boys' health

Four sessions, in order: name it — what is inside the box we put boys in; feel it — what that rigidity costs them; connect it — how those norms lead to violence; act on it — what each student will do differently.

Video unavailable offline. The four sessions are described above — move on.

Why this module is in the curriculum: at TCC in May 2025, boys were about three times more likely than girls to agree with statements that blame the victim of violence (32.1% against 11.7%). The module is written for boys and girls together.

Where we could go from here

Four things we would like to explore with you. None of them needs a decision today.

  • 01 Take part, if you would like to. One of the five groups in Phase 1 is key local informants — adults in Toledo District who work with young people. Taking part is one short survey, or one interview if you would rather talk than write. That is the whole of it, and it ends when Phase 1 ends.
  • 02 An Interact club at TCC — as an idea, not a proposal. Interact is Rotary's own programme for young people aged 12 to 18, which happens to be the ages in Forms 1 to 4. We are not putting it forward. We would like to sit down with a few of you some time and work through what it would actually involve, what it would ask of the club, and whether it suits TCC at all. It is independent of this study either way.
  • 03 Compare notes with us. We have read the research on what makes these programmes work. You know Toledo — how the school year runs against the harvest, which languages a form has to be printed in to be read, how to reach parents who will not come to a meeting at the school, and what has been tried here before. Put the two together and this gets better than either of us would manage alone.
  • 04 Later, and only if Phase 1 says proceed. If the findings support going forward, we would welcome a conversation about whether a Rotary district or global grant is a fit, given how the modules line up with the areas of focus we went through earlier. Not today.
TIME 23:00-25:00. Tone is the whole slide. Lead with the heading and mean it: none of these needs a decision today, and nothing here is money. On number one, keep it small and concrete -- one survey, or one conversation, and it finishes when Phase 1 finishes. On number two, be careful not to sound like you are selling Interact. It is their programme, not ours; we are raising it because it fits a school they already fund scholarships into, and the honest next step is a conversation about what it would really take. On number three, speak as equals, because that is what this is: we bring the research, they bring Toledo, and neither is worth much here without the other. Do not turn it into a request for correction. On number four, name it, say "not today," and move on. Then stop talking and let them respond -- this is the point in the talk where the room should be doing more of the speaking than you are.
